Macro reference — keep in mind

The macro funnel
How a currency gets strength, top to bottom:
Economy & data → central bank → interest rates → 2Y yield → currency
# and in a crisis, FEAR overrides the whole chain
Under all of it: trust.
Interest rates — the engine
The central-bank rate ripples into every currency's appeal.
Rates ↑ → better return → stronger
Rates ↓ → less reward → weaker
You track it via the 2-year yield.
Inflation & the thermostat
Inflation = speed, rates = brakes.
Inflation ↑ → raise rates → cools → stronger (if trusted)
If the bank looks helpless, the same inflation = weaker.
Bonds & yields
Price & yield move opposite — money into bonds pushes yields down.
2Y = near-term rate bets
10Y − 2Y = curve; inverted = recession warning
The two forces
RATE HUNGER (normal) → chase yield
FEAR (crisis) → chase safety → USD/CHF/JPY bid
Ask: chasing return, or safety? (regime pill up top).
Central banks — hawkish vs dovish
Hawkish = inflation-worried → higher rates → stronger.
Dovish = growth-worried → lower rates → weaker.
Read the changed words vs the last statement.
Markets trade surprises
Price moves on surprises, not news — expected = priced in.
Only Actual vs Forecast matters.
Level vs momentum
When level & momentum disagree, momentum leads (markets pay for change).
Level = where it IS · Momentum (2Y change) = where it's GOING ← trust earlier
Filter with a 5-day window; a lean, not a trigger.
Reading the data — pre-trade check
1 Rate gap direction? (bias board)
2 Surprise due? (news)
3 Fear driving? (regime)
4 Data fresh? (stale = trust less)
Bias is a filter; your chart times the entry.
